## 1.2 The scope we are proposing, stated at the front

Section 2.2 of this RFP permits Proposers to respond on a subset of functionality, provided the subset addresses at minimum one of the defined areas. iteria is responding on the **financial accounting scope**: General Ledger and Financial Reporting; Budgeting; Purchasing; Accounts Payable; and Accounts Receivable and Cash Receipting.

**iteria is not proposing the property tax collection scope** covering Tax Billing, Real Estate, Refunds and Exemptions, and Business Personal (Tangible) Property.

We want to be direct about why, because the County is entitled to know it now rather than at demonstration.

Kentucky property tax billing and collection is a specialised body of law and process. The Sheriff's Office calculates bills using rates set by multiple independent taxing jurisdictions, including the Jefferson County School District, multiple fire districts and the downtown business district; issues 350,000 bills a year against $1,236,484,560.79 in assessed value; runs a single September through April collection cycle; and distributes collected revenue to each jurisdiction on a daily or monthly basis while retaining commissions and fees. Oracle Fusion does not provide that functionality, and no amount of configuration produces it. The requirement is properly met by a Kentucky property tax product.

We would rather be scored on the half of this solicitation we can genuinely deliver than claim the whole of it and fail a scripted demonstration in October.

If the County prefers a single award, we would welcome a partnership with a tax collection provider under Section 2.2, with the prime relationship defined as the County requires. We are equally prepared to serve as the financial systems provider alongside a separately awarded tax vendor, and our approach to the interface between the two is set out in Tab 7.

The County's financial environment is well within the range we deliver: 30 funds, 3,450 general ledger lines, a four-segment chart of accounts of Fund, Department, Account and Object Code, an operating budget of $41,929,250 and a capital budget of $3,102,000, approximately 350 vendors, and 50 purchase cards in use. Check stock is printed by the Commonwealth of Kentucky, which is an interface rather than a print function.

We have implemented this exact footprint for county government. ## 2.2 What changes for the Sheriff's Office

The County currently runs an AS400 web portal for property tax collection. The financial accounting functions surrounding it are the subject of this proposal.

The specific outcomes the County has asked for map to the solution as follows.

**Timely, accurate and complete information available to citizens, County personnel and vendors.** Reporting is delivered through Oracle Transactional Business Intelligence against live transactional data, rather than through extracts. Departmental users see their own budget-to-actual position without a request to Finance.

**A secure, stable, reliable and user-friendly solution.** Quarterly updates are applied by Oracle on a published schedule. There is no version the County can fall behind on, and no end-of-support date to plan against.

**Robust integration capability, including Laserfiche.** Addressed in Tab 6.

**An enhanced online payment portal and streamlined customer experience.** Receipting and cash application on the financial side integrate to the payment channel. The citizen-facing tax payment portal itself belongs to the tax solution, and we have scoped the interface rather than the portal.

## 2.3 Chart of accounts

The County's chart of accounts is Fund, Department (Division), Account, Object Code. Oracle Fusion's accounting structure accommodates that directly and allows additional segmentation where the County wants reporting it cannot obtain today.

Chart of accounts design is the first substantive decision of the project. At Fond du Lac County, redesigning the chart of accounts was an explicit part of the work that recovered a failing implementation. We schedule it inside the first six weeks with a single named County decision owner.

# Tab 3. Project Approach and Implementation Methodology

## 3.1 Method

iteria follows a defined sequence:

1. Conduct a Boundary Confirmation session covering the County's near-term and future goals, to frame the implementation approach
2. Perform Solution Analysis sessions with the business covering requirements
3. Document and review requirements and the solution with business users
4. Obtain sign-offs and approvals from the business
5. On approval of the solution, begin development
6. Evaluate the data and the roadmap for conversion
7. Review and design interfaces
8. Conduct smoke testing
9. Conduct multiple prototype sessions
10. Support User Acceptance Testing
11. Provide Train-the-Trainer training
12. Develop and provide complete configuration documentation
13. Review final output with the business and obtain final sign-offs

Work areas are overall project management, integration point analysis, business process re-engineering, data extraction and cleansing, solution development, interface development, testing, training, go-live cutover, and post-implementation support.

We do not implement from spreadsheets and questionnaires. We hold detailed conversations and interactive design sessions. A questionnaire records what staff believe the process to be. A working session shows what actually happens during the September to April collection season when the process is inconvenient.

# Tab 6. System and Application Architecture

Oracle Fusion Cloud Applications is a multi-tenant Software as a Service platform. Application, database and infrastructure layers are operated by Oracle. The County consumes the service through a browser with no local client installation.

Quarterly updates are applied by Oracle on a published schedule with a defined testing window. We will build a County-specific regression playbook so the testing obligation fits inside that window and can be executed by County staff rather than by a consultant.

## 6.1 Integration

iteria differentiates itself by focusing on integration points. Our requirements process examines upstream and downstream dependencies for every process in scope, and interface error handling is specified at design time rather than added after a failure in production.

The integration scope for the financial systems comprises:

**Laserfiche.** The County has identified Laserfiche integration as a requirement. Document references are attached to transactions in the financial system, with the document itself remaining in Laserfiche as the system of record.

**Property tax collection system.** Whichever product the County selects, the financial interface is the daily and monthly settlement: collections received, commissions and fees retained, and distributions payable to the School District, fire districts and the downtown business district. This posts to the general ledger and to receivables. We treat this as the single most important interface in the engagement and have specified it as bi-directional with a reconciliation control rather than a one-way feed.

**Commonwealth of Kentucky check printing.** Payment files are produced for printing by the Commonwealth rather than locally, which is a file interface with a defined acknowledgement and exception path.

**Banking.** Cash receipting, deposit and reconciliation.

Interfaces are documented in an Interface Inventory during the first phase and specified individually, each with an owner, a trigger, a data contract, an error path and a reconciliation method.

# Tab 7. Data Conversion Plan

Conversion is the most common cause of overrun in public sector financial system projects, and the cause is almost always the same: agreement on who owns data cleansing comes too late.

Our proposed division of responsibility is that the County extracts source data and owns its quality, and iteria owns mapping, transformation, load and reconciliation. We provide extract specifications and validation templates in the first phase so cleansing can begin while configuration is still under way rather than after it.

Proposed conversion scope, subject to confirmation at Boundary Confirmation:

- Chart of accounts and trial balance, with opening balances by fund
- Prior year comparative balances for financial reporting
- Vendor master and 1099 history for approximately 350 vendors
- Open purchase orders and encumbrances
- Open payables
- Accounts receivable and open items
- Budget for the current and prior fiscal year

Every conversion runs at least twice before user acceptance testing, and is reconciled to a County-signed control total on each run. A conversion that has not been reconciled to a signed total has not been completed, regardless of whether it loaded without error.

The AS400 environment is the source for the tax side rather than the financial side, and we have not assumed access to it. If the County requires financial history to be extracted from the AS400 web portal, that is a scope item we would size during Boundary Confirmation rather than estimate blindly here.

# Tab 8. Security and Software Hosting

Hosting is within the contiguous United States, meeting the County's stated requirement. The County has no preference as to specific location.

Role-based access control is designed against the Sheriff's Office's actual segregation of duties, not from a generic template. For an organisation that collects and distributes public revenue, the controls that matter are the separation between receipting, distribution and reconciliation, and the audit trail behind every distribution to a taxing jurisdiction.

Encryption is applied in transit and at rest. Single sign-on integrates to the County's identity provider. Access is reviewed and recertified on a defined cycle.

Ownership of County data remains with the County throughout the term and on exit, and data is returned in a documented format at termination.

# Tab 9. Testing and Quality Assurance Plan

Testing progresses through Conference Room Pilot, then System Integration Testing, then User Acceptance Testing, supported by system unit testing, a complete end-to-end cycle, and regression testing.

**Conference Room Pilot** demonstrates the configured solution against the County's own processes using County data, with functional leads present. Exit is by sign-off from the functional leads.

**System Integration Testing** runs a minimum of two complete end-to-end cycles, including the tax settlement interface and the Commonwealth check printing file. A live defect log is triaged daily.

**User Acceptance Testing** is County-led against County-written scenarios. iteria supports rather than executes it, because acceptance testing performed by the implementer is not acceptance testing.

**Financial period close** is exercised end to end before go-live authorisation, including a full month-end close and the distribution reconciliation, with results agreed against the legacy system.

Go-live authorisation is a formal go or no-go decision against defined exit criteria, taken by the Steering Committee.

---

# Tab 10. Training Plan

Training material is built from the County's own configured system, using screenshots from the County's environment. Generic courseware teaches staff a system they do not have.

Delivery is Train-the-Trainer supported by classroom sessions, with material-only and one-to-one formats for low-volume functions. This is the structure we used at Fond du Lac County across approximately twenty departments and county functions.

For the Sheriff's Office the audiences requiring distinct treatment are Finance staff performing daily receipting and distribution, budget preparers across divisions, purchasing and accounts payable staff, and approvers who use the system occasionally and therefore need the shortest possible path through it.

Training is scheduled to avoid the peak of the September through April collection cycle wherever the go-live sequence permits.
