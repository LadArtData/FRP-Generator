## 1.2 What we would bring to Salem

Salem is a town of roughly 30,000 in Rockingham County, running a $56,686,638 operating budget and a $9,129,436 capital budget across fourteen departments with 265 full-time equivalents, on a calendar fiscal year. Those are the numbers the system has to carry, and the second one is the one currently invisible.

The Town is replacing MuniSmart, a Harris product running on premises in a legacy client-server architecture, with data held in flat file structures rather than a relational database. Around it the Town operates ClearGov for operating and personnel budgeting, EasyCIP for capital planning, and a set of manual steps that connect them. The budget is built in ClearGov, exported to Excel, and uploaded into MuniSmart, where new accounts must be reviewed and created by hand before posting. Mid-year amendments are tracked largely outside the system, often informally. Capital spending is carried as ordinary line-item activity in the operating budget, which leaves the Town without visibility into multi-year capital balances.

We have read that description carefully because it tells us what the Town is actually buying. It is not a general ledger. Salem already has one. What the Town lacks is a single system of record in which the budget is developed, approved, posted and amended without an export, a spreadsheet and a manual account build in the middle of the cycle.

That shapes our proposal. We treat the ClearGov to MuniSmart handoff as the defect to eliminate, not as an interface to reproduce.

# Tab 2. Software Solution, Data Conversion, Architecture, Security, and Hosting

## 2.1 Proposed solution

Oracle Fusion Cloud Applications, delivered as software as a service. The proposed footprint covers Financials, Human Capital Management, Payroll, Projects and Grants, and Procurement.

Salem's numbers sit comfortably inside the range we deliver. Fifteen to twenty funds and 2,200 accounts against a three-segment chart of accounts; 835 purchase orders and 150 blanket purchase orders a year; approximately 1,300 active vendors and 9,500 to 10,000 invoices; 455 fixed assets against a $25,000 capitalisation threshold with a five-year life, cruisers excepted; 17 grants, 13 donations and 16 active projects; 265 full-time equivalents; and 471 W-2s issued against a weekly payroll of 350 to 400 employees per cycle.

## 2.2 Chart of accounts

Salem runs a three-segment chart of accounts of fund, department and account. Oracle Fusion uses a flexible accounting structure that will accommodate that structure directly, and will also accommodate the additional segmentation the Town will want once capital and grant activity is tracked properly rather than as operating line items.

Chart of accounts design is the first substantive decision of the project and the one with the longest tail. At Fond du Lac County, chart of accounts redesign was an explicit part of the work that recovered a failing implementation. We schedule it in the first six weeks, with the Finance Director as the decision owner.

## 2.3 The budget cycle

This is where we expect the Town to feel the difference.

Today the budget is developed in ClearGov, capital planning runs separately in EasyCIP, personnel budgeting is maintained centrally by Finance at individual position level against pay rates, step increases, benefits and retirement rates, and one-time requests arrive as Word or PDF documents outside the system. Multiple versions are held in ClearGov through the autumn to reflect department requests, Town Manager recommendations, Town Council and Budget Committee actions. Approved figures are then exported to Excel and loaded to MuniSmart, requiring manual review and creation of new accounts before posting.

In the proposed solution, operating, personnel and capital budgeting are developed in the same system that holds the general ledger. Position budgeting is driven from the HR record, so pay rates, steps, benefit elections and retirement contributions are calculated rather than maintained separately. Budget versions are held natively, so department request, Town Manager recommendation, Council action and Budget Committee action are versions of one budget rather than separate spreadsheets. Approved budget posts to the general ledger without an export. Mid-year amendments run through a defined workflow with an audit trail.

Whether the Town retains ClearGov for public budget presentation is a Town decision. If it does, we will build the interface. Our position is that it should not be required to carry the budget into the ledger.

## 2.4 Data conversion

Conversion is the most common cause of overrun in public sector ERP projects, and the reason is almost always the same: nobody agreed early enough who owns cleansing.

Our proposed division of responsibility is that the Town extracts and owns the quality of source data, and iteria owns the mapping, transformation, load and reconciliation. We will provide extract specifications and validation templates in the first phase so that data quality work can begin while configuration is still under way rather than after it.

MuniSmart holds its data in flat file structures rather than a relational database. That is an advantage for extraction, because flat files are straightforward to read, and a risk for interpretation, because referential integrity that a database would enforce may not have been enforced in practice. We plan a data profiling exercise in the first phase specifically to establish that.

Proposed conversion scope, subject to confirmation with the Town during Boundary Confirmation: chart of accounts and balances; open purchase orders; vendor master and 1099 history; open payables; accounts receivable and outstanding invoices; fixed asset register with accumulated depreciation; grant and project master with life-to-date activity; employee master, position and salary history; benefit elections; and accrual balances.

Every conversion runs at least twice before user acceptance testing, and is reconciled to a Town-signed control total each time.

## 2.5 Architecture

Oracle Fusion Cloud Applications is a multi-tenant software as a service platform. There is no Town-side server infrastructure to procure, house or maintain, which removes the single application server and legacy client-server architecture the Town currently operates.

Quarterly updates are applied by Oracle on a published schedule with a defined testing window. We will build a Salem-specific regression playbook so that the Town's testing obligation fits inside that window and can be executed by Town staff rather than by a consultant.

## 2.6 Security

Role-based access control is designed against Salem's actual segregation of duties, not against a generic template. Encryption is applied in transit and at rest. Single sign-on integrates to the Town's identity provider.

## 2.7 Hosting

Production and non-production environments are hosted in Oracle Cloud Infrastructure commercial regions within the contiguous United States, with the primary region us-ashburn-1 and disaster recovery in us-phoenix-1. No Town data is stored or processed outside the continental United States. iteria's delivery offices include locations outside the United States; no personnel outside the continental United States will be granted access to Town production data, and we accept that as a contract term rather than a statement of intent.

# Tab 3. Implementation Methodology, Key Personnel, Training Plan, and Testing and Quality Assurance Plan

## 3.1 Implementation methodology

iteria follows a defined sequence:

1. Conduct a Boundary Confirmation session covering the Town's near-term and future goals, to frame the implementation approach
2. Perform Solution Analysis sessions with the business covering requirements
3. Document and review requirements and the solution with business users
4. Obtain sign-offs and approvals from the business
5. On approval of the solution, begin development
6. Evaluate the data and the roadmap for conversion
7. Review and design interfaces
8. Conduct smoke testing
9. Conduct multiple prototype sessions
10. Support User Acceptance Testing
11. Provide Train-the-Trainer training
12. Develop and provide complete configuration documentation
13. Review final output with the business and obtain final sign-offs

We do not implement from spreadsheets and questionnaires. We hold detailed conversations and interactive design sessions. A questionnaire captures what staff believe the process to be. A working session reveals what actually happens at the end of a month when the process is inconvenient. For a Town running a weekly payroll across Police and Fire with 155 employees needing advanced scheduling, that difference is the project.

## 3.2 Key personnel

All iteria resources are employees of the firm. We do not staff from bench, and we do not place subcontractors in client-facing roles. Consultants are selected for skill set, geographic proximity and cultural fit. Resources are certified or cross-trained across multiple modules, typically three modules up and three modules down, and all staff assigned on site are senior practitioners.

All iteria resources report to the Client Manager assigned to the engagement, who is a member of the iteria executive team. The Project Manager is a member of iteria's leadership team. Each functional area has a designated lead.

Resumes for proposed personnel are provided in the Attachment A forms in this tab.

## 3.4 Training plan

Training material is built from the Town's own configured system using screenshots from the Town's environment. Generic courseware teaches staff a system they do not have.

Delivery is Train-the-Trainer supported by classroom sessions, with material-only and one-to-one formats for low-volume functions. This is the structure we used at Fond du Lac County across approximately twenty departments.

Audiences requiring distinct treatment in Salem are Finance, the departments entering budget requests, Police and Fire on scheduling and time entry, and the payroll function on a weekly cycle.

## 3.5 Testing and quality assurance

Testing runs Conference Room Pilot, then System Integration Testing, then User Acceptance Testing, supported by system unit testing, a complete end-to-end cycle, regression testing, and payroll parallel testing.

Payroll parallel testing runs a minimum of one complete weekly cycle, with gross-to-net reconciled to the current system before go-live authorisation is sought. With 471 W-2s and 350 to 400 employees paid weekly, a single mis-set deduction code is visible to the entire workforce within seven days.

Go-live authorisation is a formal go or no-go decision against defined exit criteria, not a date.

# Tab 4. Project Schedule

The Town has stated its own dates: implementation activity beginning May 2027, financial modules live May 2028, human resources and payroll live July 2028. We have built to those dates rather than to a duration of our choosing, and we are not proposing to beat them. A schedule that arrives early on a municipal ERP is usually a schedule that has moved work onto Town staff who cannot absorb it.

Two waves, matching the Town's phasing.

| Phase | Dates | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Boundary Confirmation and Solution Analysis | May – Jul 2027 | Goals, scope boundary, requirements sessions, chart of accounts design, data profiling, interface inventory, instance strategy |
| Wave 1 build and Conference Room Pilot | Jul – Nov 2027 | Financials configuration, interface development, first conversion loads, Conference Room Pilot, change management launch |
| Wave 1 system integration testing | Nov 2027 – Jan 2028 | Two complete end-to-end cycles, defect triage, report development |
| Wave 1 acceptance and training | Feb – Apr 2028 | Town-led acceptance testing, Train-the-Trainer, end-user training |
| **Wave 1 go-live: financial modules** | **May 2028** | Cutover at the fiscal boundary, hypercare on site |
| Wave 2 build and testing | Jan – Apr 2028 | HR, payroll, benefits, applicant tracking configuration and integration; runs in parallel with Wave 1 acceptance |
| Wave 2 acceptance and payroll parallel | Apr – Jun 2028 | Town-led acceptance testing, minimum two complete weekly payroll cycles processed in parallel |
| **Wave 2 go-live: human resources and payroll** | **July 2028** | Cutover, hypercare on site, transition to support |

Two points on the sequencing. The Town's fiscal year runs 1 January to 31 December, so a May 2028 financials go-live falls mid-year. That is workable and we have planned for it, but it means the conversion carries a partial-year general ledger and the first year-end close runs on the new system with five months of converted history behind it. We size that explicitly rather than discovering it in December 2028. Second, Wave 2 acceptance ends in June and open enrolment runs mid-October to 31 October, which gives the Town a full quarter of stable payroll before benefits administration takes its first live enrolment.

Go-live is aligned to a fiscal period boundary and to a payroll cycle boundary. Cutover is a multi-day process governed by a Business Continuity Plan covering data extracts and conversion runs, planned downtime, shutdown of legacy applications, redirection of interfaces, final configuration review and the go or no-go decision.

Governance runs an Executive Steering Committee monthly, a project status meeting weekly, and technical working sessions weekly. A written status report is issued weekly at minimum, carrying a Project Health Card that rates schedule, resources, budget, risks and issues as No Issues, Caution or Critical, with live risk and issue registers recording owner, mitigation and status.

We recommend that the Town hold the project repository. We have seen many cases where the implementation partner keeps the project record on internal systems, which leaves the client without control of its own documentation. We recommend against that arrangement including where we are the partner.

# Tab 8. Exceptions to Project Scope and Contract Terms

The exception recorded in Tab 1 applies: iteria proposes on the financial and human capital scope and does not propose the Tax Collector functional area. Our Attachment B response reflects that scope and identifies the integration to the Town's selected tax solution.

We take three written exceptions to the Town's contract terms. We record them here rather than raising them after award, because the solicitation treats failure to negotiate as grounds to move to the next proposer, and a term we intend to argue about is not a term we have accepted.

**Indemnification.** The clause states that indemnification "shall not be subject to any limitations of remedies or warranties which are contained in this or any other agreement." We cannot accept uncapped indemnity. We propose indemnification capped at the greater of total fees paid under the agreement or our applicable insurance limits, with no cap on claims arising from our gross negligence, wilful misconduct, or breach of confidentiality.

**Software upgrades at no cost.** The clause entitles the Town to all upgraded software versions at no cost. iteria does not publish Oracle's software and does not control its release or pricing. We can commit that Oracle Fusion updates are included in the subscription and applied by Oracle on its published quarterly schedule at no additional licence charge, and we accept responsibility for regression testing each update against the Town's configuration. We cannot warrant Oracle's future pricing.

**Not-to-exceed and invoicing.** The agreement is not-to-exceed. Our fees are therefore fixed by phase against the gates in Tab 4 and invoiced on gate acceptance, not biweekly against elapsed time. Travel is charged at actual cost against a not-to-exceed amount stated in Attachment C. Invoices are mailed in hard copy, net 30, submitted before the twentieth, as the agreement requires.

We accept the remaining terms as written, including the insurance schedule, the requirement that all Town data be stored and processed within the continental United States, the disaster recovery and daily offsite backup obligations, the fifteen-day data return on termination, and the one hundred and eighty day offer validity period.
