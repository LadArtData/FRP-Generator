# Executive Summary

## The City's position

The City's current ERP is installed on premise and covers Account, Cashiering, Human Resources, Payroll, Reporting and Procurement Inventory. The incumbent vendor has announced end of support in 2030 for the version the City runs, and end of life for all on-premise installations. As the City's FY2027 Capital Improvements Program records, moving to the vendor's cloud product requires effort comparable to changing vendors outright. That is what created the opportunity for this competition.

Around that core the City has accumulated separate applications for budgeting, wastewater billing, property tax billing, welfare, vital records and contracts, licensing and permitting, code enforcement, land management, timekeeping and scheduling, and document management, connected by 24 interfaces documented in Table 3 of the RFP. The City's stated preference is to replace as many of these as possible.

## What we believe will decide this project

**Payroll complexity, not financials, is the critical path.** The City has sequenced finance first across 48 months, which is the correct response to the 2030 deadline. The concentration of risk, however, sits in payroll: two cycles, non-standard period counts, two workweek definitions, 48-hour Fire shifts, 471 pay codes, 440 deduction codes, approximately 26 bargaining agreements, and four payroll staff supporting more than 100 approving managers. Our plan treats payroll parallel testing as a gate rather than a task, and requires a minimum of one complete pay period processed in parallel before go-live authorisation.

**One award, two budget calendars.** The Nashua School District is a dependent district; the Board of Education governs and the Board of Aldermen approves the budget. This is one contract, one prime contractor and one award. The solution must nonetheless replace both the City's and the School Department's budgeting applications with distinct calendars and workflows, carry Student Activity Accounting and substitute and advanced scheduling, interface to the School District student information and budgeting systems, and produce DOE-25, Maintenance of Effort, Title I certification, SF425 and DRA reporting. The FY2027 consolidation of City and School information technology into a single department simplifies governance; it does not merge the two budget calendars.

**Single sign-on across four Active Directory domains is an architecture decision.** It is identified in our integration approach and carried in our pricing rather than discovered during build.

**Incumbent ancillary systems are not disclosed until short list.** The City has declined on four occasions to name the surrounding applications, and Addendum 1 moves disclosure from the successful bidder to the short-listed vendors. Estimates only are expected at submittal, with a best and final offer available after disclosure. We have therefore estimated integration effort against the functions named in Table 3 and stated our assumptions explicitly rather than concealing the uncertainty inside a number.

## Why iteria

iteria delivers exclusively on the Oracle platform. We have implemented the full Oracle Fusion suite, covering Financials, Human Capital Management, Payroll, Projects and Enterprise Asset Management, for county governments of comparable staff size and organisational complexity, as prime contractor, without subcontractors, replacing JD Edwards and Tyler Munis. We have recovered a public-sector Fusion implementation that was failing and delivered it on schedule. Our consultants are cross-trained across multiple modules, which is what allows a team sized appropriately for an organisation of 3,500 employees to cover 66 departments without a handoff at every functional boundary.

# Project Understanding

## Scope as we understand it

The City seeks a fully integrated, cloud-based enterprise resource planning solution embedding sound business practice, covering Financials, Payroll, Human Resources, Tax Billing and Utility Billing, with Document Management, Licensing and Permitting, Event Management and Timekeeping identified as additional areas of interest. The solution will serve all City departments including the School District, Department of Public Works, Police, Fire and Library.

We note the City's guidance that Licensing and Permitting is likely to fall in Phase II or a later phase, and that the Appendix A requirements worksheets are weighted, scored, subject to verification at any point in the procurement, and that misrepresentation of product functionality is grounds for disqualification. Our responses in those worksheets are made against actual Oracle Fusion capability.

## The decisions that will shape the first ninety days

**Chart of accounts.** With 5,480 account units, 450 active funds, 66 departments, one legal entity, one tax identification number and one bank, the chart of accounts redesign is the foundational decision of the programme. At Fond du Lac County, chart of accounts redesign was an explicit component of the re-baseline that recovered the project.

**Payroll.** Two payroll cycles, non-standard pay period counts, two workweek definitions, 48-hour Fire shifts, 471 pay codes, 440 deduction codes, 1,200 job rate classes, approximately 26 collective bargaining agreements, and New Hampshire Retirement System reporting comprising an XML upload and monthly posting.

**Utility billing.** Wastewater only. Consumption data arrives from Pennichuck Water Works as a manual weekly file, and solid waste and septic charges are billed from the same water usage data. That manual dependency is both a design constraint and a candidate for automation in its own right.

**Tax billing.** 28,900 bills issued twice yearly under the standard New Hampshire RSA process with no modifications, together with a CAMA extract from the Assessing system supplying property characteristics.

**Timekeeping.** 46 physical clocks using PIN and proximity credentials rather than biometrics; approximately 400 employees requiring advanced scheduling; no shift or vacation bidding required.

**Data conversion.** Five years of transactional financial data. The City will export flat files and retains ownership of data cleansing. We will hold that boundary in the responsibility matrix, because uncontrolled conversion scope is the most common cause of overrun in public-sector ERP programmes.

## Our role as we understand it

The City expects the prime contractor to carry the solution, to provide a dedicated Project Manager for the full duration replaceable only with City approval, and to participate in a three-day on-site demonstration if short-listed. We accept each of these conditions.

# Project Work Plan and Schedule

## Implementation approach

iteria follows a defined step-by-step implementation method:

1. Conduct a Boundary Confirmation session covering the City's near-term and future goals, to frame the overall implementation approach
2. Perform Solution Analysis sessions with the business covering requirements
3. Document and review requirements and the solution with business users
4. Obtain sign-offs and approvals from the business
5. On approval of the solution, begin development
6. Evaluate the data and the roadmap for conversion
7. Review and design interfaces
8. Conduct smoke testing
9. Conduct multiple prototype sessions
10. Support User Acceptance Testing
11. Provide Train-the-Trainer training
12. Develop and provide complete configuration documentation
13. Review final output with the business and obtain final sign-offs

We do not implement from spreadsheets and questionnaires. We hold detailed conversations and interactive design sessions. A questionnaire records what staff believe the process to be; a working session reveals what actually happens when the process is inconvenient.

## Work areas

Overall project management; review of key decisions and integration points arising from requirements sessions; business process re-engineering; data extraction and cleansing; solution development; interface review and development; testing; training; go-live cutover; and post-implementation support.

## Testing

Testing progresses through Conference Room Pilot, System Integration Testing and User Acceptance Testing, supported by system unit testing, a complete end-to-end test cycle, regression testing, and payroll parallel testing.

Payroll parallel testing runs for a minimum of one complete pay period across both the weekly and biweekly cycles before go-live authorisation is sought. Given the number of pay codes, deduction codes and bargaining agreements in scope, this is treated as a gate rather than a task.

## Deliverables

Project Boundary Confirmation Document; Business Requirements Documents; Interface Inventory; Data Conversion Strategy; Instance Strategy; Configuration Cookbooks; Interface Specification Documents; Conference Room Pilot test scripts; conversion templates; Change Impact Assessment; Stakeholder Communication Plan; System Integration Test Plan and executed scripts; defect log; report specifications; User Acceptance Test completion sign-off; training materials; training completion report; Cutover Plan; Go-Live Authorization; Production Confirmation.

## Go-live cutover

Cutover is a multi-day, 24-hour process requiring a detailed Business Continuity Plan and closely coordinated execution. It addresses data extracts and conversion processes, adherence to the Business Continuity Plan, planned downtime, shutdown of legacy applications, redirection of interfaces, 24-hour facility access, final configuration review, and a formal go/no-go decision. Go-live is aligned to a pay cycle boundary.

# System Description

## Platform

The proposed solution is Oracle Fusion Cloud Applications, delivered as software as a service. Addendum 1 confirms Oracle as an acceptable platform. All production data is hosted within the United States.

## Architecture and security

The solution is delivered from Oracle's cloud infrastructure with role-based access control, encryption in transit and at rest, and configuration managed through Oracle's standard quarterly release cycle.

## Integration approach

iteria differentiates itself by focusing on integration points. Our requirements process examines upstream and downstream dependencies for every process in scope, and interface error handling is designed at specification time rather than retrofitted after failure.

For the City, the integration scope comprises the 24 interfaces documented in Table 3, together with:

- Single sign-on across four separate Active Directory domains, treated as an architecture activity
- New Hampshire Retirement System XML upload and monthly posting
- Pennichuck Water Works consumption data, currently a manual weekly file and a candidate for automation
- Assessing and CAMA extract supplying property characteristics to tax billing
- Bi-directional timekeeping integration across 46 physical clocks
- Bank positive pay in both directions, two lockbox interfaces, and credit card processing
- School District student information system and School District budgeting system
- Risk management, fuel, scale house, welfare, dog licensing and document management

Where the City elects to replace an ancillary system rather than interface to it, the corresponding interface is removed from scope and the effort redirected to configuration.

## Upgrade-safe configuration

We extend Oracle's applications through configuration rather than customisation wherever possible, preserving the upgrade path. For an organisation that has just been compelled to leave an on-premise platform by vendor end-of-life, remaining on the standard upgrade path is the principal reason to move to software as a service.

We will establish a quarterly patch playbook with the City so that regression testing can be completed inside Oracle's two-week window, and our method includes techniques for making that testing efficient.

## Embedded artificial intelligence

Addendum 1 requires that embedded artificial intelligence within the proposed solution must not contact external resources. Our response to this requirement is stated in the Appendix A worksheets and is based on documented Oracle product behaviour for the proposed release.

# Software Maintenance, Updates and Support

## Oracle release management

Oracle Fusion Cloud Applications are updated on a quarterly release cycle, with a defined testing window before each release is applied to production. Our approach establishes a City-specific regression playbook so that the testing burden falls within that window and is repeatable by City staff rather than dependent on the implementation partner.

## Post-implementation support

Immediately following go-live, the implementing team remains engaged through hypercare. Our standard pattern is full on-site presence during the initial stabilisation period, moving to remote support as issue volume falls. The team that implemented the system performs the support, so that knowledge of the City's configuration decisions stays with the people answering the calls.

## Managed services

iteria provides ongoing managed services following stabilisation, covering functional and technical support, quarterly release regression, configuration change management and enhancement delivery.
